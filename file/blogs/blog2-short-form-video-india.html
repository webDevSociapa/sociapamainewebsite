India is now the world's largest short-form video market. Here's what the data says — and what every brand should be doing differently right now.

Short-form video is no longer a content trend — it's become India's primary medium for brand discovery, entertainment, and purchase decisions. With 462 million users and growing, it has permanently rewritten the rules of how brands communicate.

PwC's Global Entertainment & Media Outlook 2026–30 confirms online video is India's fastest-growing ad format, projected to grow at a 15.3% CAGR to USD 3.8 billion by 2030. FICCI-EY 2026 shows India produced 200,000 hours of original video content in 2025 — the majority in short-form or episodic formats. ET Brand Equity–Ipsos reports that 29% of all digital ad spend in India now goes to online video, second only to social media.

The driving force? India's smartphone users have crossed 806 million, with the average person spending 7.3 hours daily on digital media. Short-form video — specifically Reels (60 seconds) and Shorts (up to 60 seconds) — accounts for the dominant share of this consumption time, particularly in the 18–34 age demographic that represents India's largest spending cohort.

Key Insight

68% of Gen Z consumers in India say they discover new brands primarily through short-form video. Creator-led content drives 3.2× higher purchase intent compared to traditional brand ads in the same format. — ET Brand Equity–Ipsos 2026

Trend 01 · Platform Data

Instagram Reels vs. YouTube Shorts: Which Platform Really Wins in India?

390M vs. 325M users · $1.70B vs. $0.37B ad spend · Same audience, radically different ROI

Sensor Tower's data makes the gap stark: Instagram commands USD 1.70 billion in annual ad spend in India while YouTube captures just USD 0.37 billion — yet YouTube users spend 72 minutes daily vs. Instagram's 32 minutes. This is the single biggest arbitrage opportunity in Indian digital marketing today.

India is Instagram's #1 global market with 390 million monthly active users. Reels now accounts for 58% of all time spent on Instagram in India. For brands: Instagram delivers higher purchase intent, YouTube delivers higher reach at lower cost.

Trend 02 · Ad Performance

Why Short-Form Video Ads Outperform Every Other Format

2.5× brand recall · 23% higher CTR · 3.2× purchase intent with creator-led content

ET Brand Equity–Ipsos data is unambiguous: short-form video ads deliver 2.5× higher brand recall vs. static display ads among India's 18–34 demographic. This gap widens to 3.1× for ads under 15 seconds with a strong hook in the first 3 seconds.

Data Point

Reels ads with text overlay in the first 1.5 seconds achieve 40% higher completion rates. Sound-off optimisation is non-negotiable for India — over 68% of Reels are watched without sound. — ET Brand Equity–Ipsos 2026

Creator-led short-form content drives 3.2× higher purchase intent vs. brand-produced ads. 68% of Gen Z discovers brands through short-form video first. When a creator showcases a product in a Reel, 42% of viewers check out the brand's profile within 24 hours.

YouTube Shorts offers the most undervalued inventory in Indian digital media. With USD 0.37B in ad spend vs. 325M users and 72 minutes daily watch time, Shorts delivers 40–60% lower CPM vs. comparable Reels placements.

Trend 03 · Regional Languages

The Regional Language Revolution: Hindi Is Not Enough Anymore

56M new users from non-metros · Tamil & Telugu Reels growing 3× faster · 56% of OTT in regional languages

FICCI-EY 2026 reveals that 56 million new internet users who came online in 2025 are primarily from Tier 2–4 cities — and they primarily consume content in their native languages. Regional language OTT content grew from 27% in 2020 to 56% in 2025.

Dentsu-E4M data shows that Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Bengali short-form content creators grew their follower bases at 3× the national average in 2025. Regional language Reels with vernacular copy outperform Hindi equivalents by 45–65% in engagement rate in those markets.
