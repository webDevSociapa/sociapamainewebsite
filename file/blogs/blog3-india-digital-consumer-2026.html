806 Million & Counting: The Indian Digital Consumer Is Unlike Anyone the World Has Seen

Multilingual. Mobile-first. Living between Tier 1 luxury and Tier 4 aspiration. Here's the data-backed portrait of India's 806 million digital consumers in 2026.

India's digital consumer cannot be described by a single persona. They are 806 million people who are simultaneously the most connected and most diverse digital audience on Earth — speaking 22+ scheduled languages, spread across 6 lakh villages and metro megacities, with purchase intent ranging from ₹99 app subscriptions to ₹10 lakh luxury goods.

ET Brand Equity–Ipsos's State of Digital Marketing 2025–26 gives us the clearest picture yet: India's 806 million internet users spend an average of 7.3 hours per day on digital media — more than any other major economy per user. Mobile is the primary device, driving 78% of all digital consumption. Social media accounts for 29.8% of time spent, online video 29%, and search 19%.

What's new in 2026 is where the growth is coming from. PwC's India outlook confirms that 56 million new users came online in 2025 — and the overwhelming majority are from Tier 2, Tier 3, and Tier 4 cities and rural India. These users primarily consume content in regional languages, primarily on Android smartphones under ₹15,000, and are driving the most dramatic shift in Indian consumer behaviour since the Jio revolution of 2016.

Key Insight

India's digital consumer spends more time online than Americans (7.3 hrs vs. 7.1 hrs/day). But India's ad-to-GDP ratio is still just 0.41% vs. USA's 1.4%. The implication: India is undermonetised relative to its digital engagement, and this gap will close dramatically by 2030. — PwC + ET Brand Equity 2026

Trend 01 · Time Spent Online

How Do India's 806 Million Users Actually Spend Their Time Online?

Social 29.8% · Video 29% · Search 19% · E-commerce 11% · Mobile drives 78% of all digital

ET Brand Equity–Ipsos data gives us the most granular breakdown of Indian online behaviour to date. Social media and online video together account for nearly 60% of all digital time. This is not just an entertainment story — social media is now the primary product discovery channel, and video is the dominant path-to-purchase format.

Mobile drives 78% of all digital ad spend, confirming that India's dominant digital device is the sub-₹15,000 Android smartphone. Apps command 87% of mobile time vs. 13% for mobile browsers — making app store presence and in-app advertising essential rather than supplementary.

Trend 02 · Tier 2–4 Opportunity

The Next 100 Million Customers Live in India's Smaller Cities

56M new non-metro users · Regional language-first · Growing faster than metros

India's next wave of digital consumers is arriving from Tier 2, 3, and 4 cities — and they behave differently from metro audiences. FICCI-EY 2026 data shows these 56 million new users primarily consume content in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Marathi, Bengali, or other regional languages. They are price-sensitive, trust-driven buyers who rely heavily on peer recommendation, regional influencers, and word-of-mouth validation.

Dentsu-E4M reports that e-commerce ad spend grew 55.86% YoY in FY25, with a significant portion of this growth driven by Tier 2–3 city demand — particularly in categories like fashion, FMCG, home goods, and consumer electronics.

Brand Strategy Implication

Brands that have only metro-language creative (English + Hindi) are invisible to 35–40% of India's growing digital user base. The investment in regional language content in 2026 is not optional — it's the difference between reaching 500M or 800M users.

Trend 03 · Multi-Screen Behaviour

From Mobile to Living Room: India's Multi-Screen Revolution

40M CTV homes today · 67M by 2028 · Smart TV + phone simultaneous consumption

India's digital consumer is not just mobile-first — they're becoming multi-screen. PwC's outlook documents the emergence of a Connected TV (CTV) segment: 40 million smart TV homes in 2025, growing to 67 million by 2028. CTV viewers simultaneously use smartphones 73% of the time while watching TV, creating a dual-screen retargeting opportunity unlike anything available in traditional broadcast.

FICCI-EY data confirms that live events also surged: the live events sector grew 44% in 2025 as post-pandemic pent-up demand combined with IPL, concerts, and experiential brand activations drove total revenue to INR 128 billion.
